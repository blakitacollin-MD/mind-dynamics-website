About the BLAK approach and guided intervention

BLAK applies a task-oriented performance lens: the athlete’s outcome provides direction, while attention and practice return to the skills they can use now. Craig Manning’s TEDx talk describes how seeking outside approval and fixating on results can draw attention away from the task; a task-oriented approach emphasizes the skills needed to execute.

This does not make winning, scores, or other results unimportant. It treats them as outcomes to learn from, while the intervention targets the repeatable preparation, attention, decision, execution, and recovery skills that influence performance.

Key intervention: a practiced task reset

  1. Notice: Name the distraction neutrally—outcome worry, comparison, or a mistake—without arguing with the thought.
  2. Reset: Use a brief, practiced breath or familiar reset routine appropriate to the sport and moment.
  3. Re-engage: Ask, “What is my next useful and safe task?” Choose a rehearsed cue, decision, or routine step. When conditions call for it, pausing, changing the plan, or stopping is the task.
  4. Review: Track cue use, time to re-engage, and context alongside performance outcomes. The goal is not perfect focus or a guaranteed result.

BLAK turns this into an athlete-guided intervention by defining an observable target, establishing a baseline, selecting supportive strategies, and reviewing whether they help.
